# The four September strategies on the full universe

*2 October 2026. Research only. Nothing here was executed and nothing here is financial advice.*

528 instruments (500 US shares, 8 currency pairs, 20 futures) · about nine years of daily bars · 129,960 signals · all sixteen daily strategies replayed together so old and new are measured on identical data.

---

## The answer

**Three of the four add nothing. One is worth a second look. None should be switched on yet.**

- `pead_drift`, `qmj_factor` and `macro_sector_rotation` earn what simply being invested in a rising market earned, and less of it than buy-and-hold with about the same drawdown.
- `activist_13d_tracking` is the exception: fewer trades, the smallest drawdown of the four, and a risk-adjusted result in line with the best existing strategy. The evidence is thin (411 signals in nine years) and it is not proof of an edge.
- Buy-and-hold of the same universe still beat every strategy on return, as it did in August.

## 2. Per trade, against the baseline

`macro_sector_rotation` buys anything whenever the index is in an uptrend. That makes it the baseline: what an average long trade with a stop earned over this period, with no selection at all. A strategy only shows something if it beats that.

| Strategy | Signals | Average result per trade | Against the baseline, month by month |
|---|---|---|---|
| `macro_sector_rotation` (the baseline) | 39,605 | +0.18R | — |
| `qmj_factor` | 18,211 | +0.20R | 0.03R **below**, not distinguishable |
| `pead_drift` | 2,604 | +0.23R | 0.02R **below**, not distinguishable |
| `activist_13d_tracking` | 411 | +0.45R | 0.25R above |

R is the amount risked on a trade; +0.18R means the average trade made 18% of what it risked, before costs. For reference the existing strategies sit between +0.14R and +0.27R: every long rule made money in this decade.

The last column compares each strategy with the baseline in the same calendar month and averages the differences. Trades in one month move together, so the month is the honest unit of evidence, not the trade. On that test `qmj_factor` and `pead_drift` are no better than buying anything at all in an uptrend; their higher per-trade averages come from trading in better months.

The activist proxy is ahead by 0.25R over 94 months, about 2.6 standard errors. On its own that would count as evidence. But four ideas were tried here, and with four tries the bar for the best of them is about 2.5. So it is borderline: enough to justify one clean test on new data, not enough to trade on. (For scale, the same test puts `ts_momentum` at 2.5 and `long_reversal` at 1.8.)

## 3. The four, one at a time

**`macro_sector_rotation` — keep off.** It is exposure to the market with a stop-loss. 10.2% a year against 22.9% for holding, with a 34.7% drawdown against 37.7%. It also signals on almost every instrument, so switched on it would crowd out everything else.

**`qmj_factor` — keep off.** "Price more than 5% above its 50-day average" is a trend rule, and it behaves like the trend rules already in the library, a little worse than `ts_momentum`. Its returns move 0.74 with `ts_momentum`'s. It would be a second copy of something you already have.

**`pead_drift` — keep off.** A big up-day on heavy volume, held for a few weeks. 12.4% a year with a 36.7% drawdown, which is buy-and-hold's risk for half its return. A real earnings-drift test needs earnings dates; this cannot tell an earnings surprise from any other spike.

**`activist_13d_tracking` — the one to test further.** It has nothing to do with activists. What it finds is a share whose 14-day RSI jumps from below 40 to above 60 in a single session: a sharp reversal out of weakness. It fires rarely, takes nearly every signal it gets (so its result does not depend on which names happened to be picked), and held up in both halves. Against that: 411 signals is a small sample, the first half was much stronger than the second, and it is the best of four ideas tried, which is how false discoveries are made.

## 4. What this run exposed about the study itself

**The published numbers depend on which shares get picked first.** Every strategy targets a fixed multiple of its stop, so on a rebalance day hundreds of candidates tie, and the tie is broken by the order of the universe list, which is most liquid first. With a dozen slots that order decides what is held. Scoring the same trades in alphabetical order instead moved `ts_momentum` from 18.5% a year to 10.2%, and `low_beta` from 8.3% to 1.8%. So part of each published return is "the largest US companies did best this decade" rather than the rule. This applies to the August table too.

**Under today's settings the backtest sits mostly in cash.** Since August the portfolio settings moved from about a dozen positions of up to 15% to positions of 1% with at most 25 per market. All 500 shares count as one market, so the backtest can never have more than about a quarter of the book in shares. Scored that way:

| Strategy | Return a year | Worst drawdown | Sharpe | Trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Buy and hold (fully invested)* | 22.9% | 37.7% | 1.00 | — |
| `long_reversal` | 7.3% | 5.1% | 1.57 | 1,188 |
| `ts_momentum` | 7.2% | 9.5% | 1.15 | 3,482 |
| **`qmj_factor`** (new) | 5.7% | 9.7% | 1.01 | 3,017 |
| **`macro_sector_rotation`** (new) | 5.0% | 12.3% | 0.81 | 5,779 |
| **`pead_drift`** (new) | 3.2% | 8.2% | 0.70 | 2,106 |
| **`activist_13d_tracking`** (new) | 1.0% | 2.0% | 0.90 | 411 |
| All sixteen together | 8.2% | 16.1% | 0.96 | 8,381 |

Those figures are low because the book is three-quarters cash, not because the rules stopped working. The live paper book does not apply the per-market limit and ran at 90% invested, so **the backtest and the live book are no longer the same portfolio**. That needs settling before any backtest number is used to judge the live book.

---

## 5. What I would do next

1. Leave all four switched off. Nothing here justifies changing what the paper book holds.
2. Test the activist proxy on data it has not seen: the UK and European universe, which this study does not touch. One clean test, no tuning. If it fails there, drop it.
3. Decide what the backtest's portfolio settings should be, so that it describes the book the paper trader actually runs.
4. Replace the liquidity tie-break with something deliberate, or report results as a range across several orderings.

## How it was run

```
STUDY_UNIVERSE=reports/share/universe.csv \
STUDY_ENABLE=pead_drift,qmj_factor,macro_sector_rotation,activist_13d_tracking \
STUDY_TAG=OCT02 STUDY_WORKERS=8 python tools/full_study.py
```

- The same 528 instruments as the published study, read from its own universe file.
- The four new strategies were switched on for this run only. `config.yaml` still has them off.
- One replay, 48 minutes on eight processes. The parallel replay was checked against the single-process one and produced identical trades in identical order.
- Everything is written with an `-OCT02` suffix. The published study files and the edge file the live book sizes from were not touched.
- The replay covered all 528 instruments. The scoring afterwards priced 515: thirteen currency and futures series came back too short through the broker feed once Trader Workstation was switched on mid-run. Their trades are still counted, and buy-and-hold is measured on the 515.
- Section 1 re-scores the saved trades under the August portfolio settings: `reports/FULL-STUDY-OCT02-published-settings.json`. Section 4's table is the run under today's settings: `reports/FULL-STUDY-OCT02.json`.

The caveats in `reports/share/METHOD.md` all still apply: in-sample, survivorship bias, futures sized at full notional, one market, one decade.
